01

Overview

A team project asks whether counterfactual actions suggested by an employee-attrition model are equally achievable for different demographic groups.

Using the IBM HR Attrition dataset, the analysis goes beyond prediction parity to examine the effectiveness, cost and choice of recommended changes.

02

Requirements & constraints

An action can flip a model prediction without being realistic for a person. Immutable features, permitted ranges and salary-change limits therefore matter.

The analysis compares gender, age and marital-status groups. Group size, classifier threshold and the recourse cost definition affect the apparent gaps.

03

My contribution

I led fairness and FACTS analysis, results interpretation and visualization. I examined prediction disparities and how salary constraints change access to recourse.

04

Approach & decisions

Random Forest and XGBoost supply predictions; DiCE generates candidate counterfactual changes for people classified as at risk.

The analysis examines group prediction metrics, feature-flip sensitivity and FACTS-inspired recourse comparisons: effectiveness, cost, effectiveness within budget and number of options.

A separate sensitivity experiment tightens the salary cap from +50% to +35% and +20%. Keeping this separate allows the baseline recourse generation to be reused.

Shared utilities align paths, model wrappers, feature constraints and recourse output tables across local and Colab execution.

05

Results & validation

The project README reports high recourse availability under the explored caps, while cost and within-budget comparisons expose differences that availability alone can hide.

Its sensitivity summary reports a larger XGBoost effectiveness gap under tighter salary limits: for marital status, 0.017 at the loose cap versus 0.034 at the tight cap. Random Forest is reported as more stable in this comparison.

Saved notebooks, subgroup tables and plots make these comparisons inspectable. The original recourse-cost figure below illustrates the type of result produced.

06

Limitations & next steps

This is an academic audit of a benchmark dataset, not a validated HR retention system. Changing model inputs does not prove that the proposed action prevents real attrition.

Protected-feature flips measure model sensitivity; without a causal model they do not establish causal counterfactual fairness.

Some documented analyses use the full available population rather than only held-out examples. Detailed claims require checking each output population, sample size and evaluation split.
