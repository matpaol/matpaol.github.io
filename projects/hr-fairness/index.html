01

Overview

A model may offer a route to a different prediction while making that route harder for some groups. This project studies that question on the IBM HR attrition dataset.

02

Requirements & constraints

Counterfactual changes were constrained by immutable features and salary-change caps. The chosen cost definition affects the interpretation of fairness.

03

My contribution

My documented role was fairness and FACTS analysis, results and visualization. Preprocessing, predictive models and the DiCE pipeline were shared team work.

04

Approach & decisions

We compared Random Forest and XGBoost through prediction, feature-change sensitivity and recourse metrics. The analysis examines effectiveness, cost and access within a fixed budget.

05

Results & validation

The notebook contains saved tables and plots comparing subgroups. A salary-cap sensitivity analysis explores how results change under tighter constraints. The figure below is an original project output.

06

Limitations & next steps

This is an academic audit, not a deployed HR system. Feature-flip sensitivity is not causal validation; conclusions depend on the data, cost model and subgroup sample sizes.
