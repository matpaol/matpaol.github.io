Fault diagnosis on a multiphase plant

Detecting and classifying faults in an experimental water–air plant — and proving the model learned the fault, not the operating point it was recorded at.

01

The problem

An experimental plant moves water and air through an ejector, logging 8 sensors and the internal signals of 3 PID controllers at 10 Hz. The public dataset has 67 recordings: 46 healthy ones over 45 operating regimes, and 21 faulty ones. I studied three faults — water-inlet obstruction, air-outlet obstruction and an air leak — and had to both detect them and tell them apart.

02

The trap: learning the operating point

Every fault was recorded at the same reference regime. Since the controller signals reveal the regime, a classifier can look accurate by learning ‘this is the reference regime’ instead of ‘this is a fault’. I tested for it directly: a detector trained on all regimes flags 52.6% of healthy samples at the reference regime as faulty, against 1.1% at the other 44 regimes.

03

Method

  • Leakage-free evaluation: adjacent 10 Hz samples are nearly identical, so random splits would be wildly optimistic. Recordings are cut into 10-second blocks with a 2-second gap discarded at each boundary; a consecutive-window split is a second check.
  • Features: 8 sensors + PID signals, minus two near-constant variables → 27 features.
  • Models: a Random Forest conditioned on the regime versus a global one, for binary detection and for multiclass diagnosis; Local Outlier Factor as a one-class alternative.
  • Interpretation: feature importance, confusion matrices and LOF score distributions to explain why each approach works or fails.

04

Results

With the alarm threshold chosen on validation data, the regime-conditioned detector finds 502–515 of 523 faulty test samples across three seeds with zero false alarms on 798 healthy ones; the multiclass model reaches 0.973 ± 0.007 accuracy. The global detector misses 163–171 faults and raises 49–58 false alarms.

LOF did not work, and the reason was informative: the fault states were locally more compact than normal operation, the opposite of what a density-based outlier detector assumes.

All faults come from one regime and one severity, and training and test intervals come from the same recordings — so this shows a sound method on this dataset, not generalization to new fault repetitions or other regimes.
