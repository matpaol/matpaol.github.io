01

Overview

My research internship at the Royal Military Academy in Brussels is part of DREAM. The work explores manipulation in cluttered scenes and how physical interactions can inform action selection.

The uploaded archive contains successive designs and prototypes. The concrete MuJoCo implementation documented here builds and measures reproducible piles of objects; the wider research direction includes reasoning, robot execution and eventual physical validation.

02

Requirements & constraints

An object may support or obstruct another. Geometric contact is not sufficient evidence of a causal dependency: removal experiments are needed.

Scene generation must be reproducible, and initial releases should avoid artificial interpenetration. Physics parameters and their uncertainty need explicit treatment.

The long-term architecture must distinguish observing the scene, deciding on an executable action and executing it. Physical validation remains a separate milestone.

03

My contribution

I develop simulation experiments during my research internship: scene generation, configurable runs, physics diagnostics and technical documentation.

04

Approach & decisions

Phase 0A constructs scenes from object catalogs, ground definitions and scenario rules. A seed determines geometry, release grouping and sampled physical properties.

The scene is converted to MJCF and simulated in MuJoCo until a settling criterion or timeout is reached. Scene descriptions and diagnostic reports are saved for reproduction.

The robotic-removal prototype randomizes an internal weight, deriving the resulting center of mass and inertia. Materials and friction values are initial hypotheses rather than measurements of real objects.

Validation includes free-fall comparison, valid inertia checks, incline/friction tests and multi-seed diagnostics. The broader observe–decide–execute architecture places robot feasibility checks before execution.

05

Results & validation

The archive documents configurable scene generation, headless and viewer execution, saved scene descriptions, physical checks and a benchmark command over 200 seeds.

One prototype README reports that 12 of the 200 scenes exceed a final penetration threshold of 1 mm. This is a useful diagnostic limitation, not a solved robustness result.

Gymnasium removal tasks, strategic decision-making, robot deployment and sim-to-real validation appear at different stages across versions. No completed physical transfer result is claimed here.

06

Limitations & next steps

The internship is ongoing, and the uploaded README snapshots may precede the latest training work. Their results should not be treated as the current final state.

A stable simulated pile is not proof of correct real-world interaction. Material calibration, causal removal experiments and physical validation remain important.

Different source folders are versions of the same research effort, not separate portfolio projects.
