01

Overview

A six-person Responsible Data Science project uses the Adult income dataset to study prediction quality alongside fairness, privacy and explainability.

The study treats a classifier as more than an accuracy score: it asks who receives errors, how mitigation changes outcomes and what explanations reveal about decisions.

02

Requirements & constraints

The income classes are imbalanced, so overall accuracy must be read alongside minority-class precision and recall.

Fairness depends on the protected attribute, subgroup definition and metric. Privacy mechanisms introduce an additional accuracy–privacy trade-off.

03

My contribution

As part of a six-person team, I contributed to a responsible machine-learning study covering predictive performance, fairness, privacy and explainability. Results below describe our shared pipeline.

04

Approach & decisions

After removing rows with missing categorical values, the dataset contains 30,162 records. The documented split uses 21,716 training, 2,413 validation and 6,033 test examples.

A scikit-learn pipeline combines numerical scaling, categorical one-hot encoding and logistic regression. Class-specific evaluation complements the confusion matrix.

The team examines Equal Opportunity Difference across sex, age and intersectional groups and compares reweighing strategies.

Further experiments investigate privacy and fairness together, local explanations with LIME, counterfactuals with MACE and global relationships through additional explanatory analyses.

05

Results & validation

The report records 85.35% test accuracy for the baseline logistic regression. Recall is 0.93 for the majority income class and 0.61 for the higher-income class.

This gap motivates the fairness and explanation work: a strong aggregate score can coexist with substantially different errors across classes and subgroups.

The deliverables include the report, executable notebook versions, model comparisons and explanation figures.

06

Limitations & next steps

This is a dataset study, not a deployed decision system. Fairness metrics do not establish causal fairness or justify using the model for consequential decisions.

Age definitions and preprocessing descriptions vary between parts of the report. Those details must be reconciled before publishing detailed subgroup comparisons.

Team-wide results should not be presented as an individually authored end-to-end implementation.
